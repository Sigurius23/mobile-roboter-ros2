the state: six numbers

x, y, heading θ, speed v, turn rate ω, acceleration a. C++ counts from 0, so x(3) is v.

WHY COS AND SIN

The speed points along the heading; cos θ and sin θ split it into an x part and a y part. A starts as the identity: what isn’t set stays the same.

WHY Eu × dt

The longer we predict without a measurement, the less sure we can be.

Predict belongs to the Kalman filter. Every callback calls it first, to move the state forward to the time of its message; only then does it correct the state with the measurement. The state is six numbers: position x and y, the heading, the speed, the turn rate and the acceleration. Predict asks: if the robot keeps moving like this, where is it after dt seconds, and how much less sure are we? These lines fill the matrix A. Position x grows by the cosine of the heading, times speed, times dt; y grows by the sine. The acceleration adds one half a dt squared, the school physics formula. The heading grows by turn rate times dt and the speed by acceleration times dt. Everything else stays the same, because A starts as the identity. Eu is the process noise: how wrong the model may be per second. It’s multiplied by dt, because the longer we predict without a measurement, the less sure we can be. Question: why is predict called in every callback? Every measurement belongs to its own moment, so the state is moved to that moment first; that’s why all sensors share one filter_time_.
