Where the scan comes from

The Ouster gives a 3D point cloud. pointcloud_to_laserscan cuts a horizontal slice and turns it into the 2D scan our node reads.

parametervalue and meaning
target_frameos_lidar: heights from the lidar
min / max_height2.0 / 6.0 m above the lidar
angle_increment0.0087 rad = 0.5°, ~722 rays
range_min / max0.45 m (the robot) / 50 m
use_infTrue: empty rays come as inf

WHY 2 TO 6 M

2.5–6.5 m above ground: facades, roofs and tree tops stay put; people and vehicles below 2 m move.

What if you change the slice?

Higher min_height: fewer moving objects, but fewer points. Lower max_height: cuts leafy tree tops, which change every scan.

The Ouster lidar gives a three-D point cloud. A node called pointcloud_to_laserscan cuts a horizontal slice out of it and turns it into the two-D scan our node reads; its parameters are in the launch file. Heights are measured from the lidar’s own frame. The slice goes from two to six metres above the lidar, which is two and a half to six and a half metres above the ground: facades, roofs and tree tops, but not people or cars, which move. The angle step is 0.0087 radians, half a degree, about seven hundred and twenty rays. The range limits: forty five centimetres cuts off the robot itself, and fifty metres is the far limit. use_inf reports empty rays as infinity, which our loop skips. Testat question: why start the slice at two metres, and what if you change it? Below two metres people and vehicles move. A higher minimum removes more moving objects but leaves fewer points; a lower maximum cuts off leafy tree tops, which change shape every scan.
