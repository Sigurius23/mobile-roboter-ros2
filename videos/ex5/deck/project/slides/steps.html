One round has seven steps. Step one pairs every old point with its nearest new point, keeping only pairs closer than one metre. Steps two and three compute the centres of both point sets, and how the shapes are turned against each other. Step four finds the best rotation and shift. Steps five and six move the old points and add the step to the total. Step seven stops when the step is tiny. The loop runs at most twenty rounds; it usually stops earlier, and twenty only caps the time per scan, since a new scan comes every fifty milliseconds. If fewer than three pairs are left, the loop stops, because the rotation and shift can’t be determined reliably.
