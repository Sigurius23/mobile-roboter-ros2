WHY JOSEPH FORM

Stays symmetric and positive even with rounding errors.

The variances go into Ez: sigma squared. Then Ez is divided by icp_gain. A gain of two halves the variance, so ICP counts twice as much. In C++, slash-equals means divide and store. On the bag, sigma v is about three metres per second, so the speed comes from the wheels, while ICP still counts for the turn rate. The update is the usual Kalman correction: S is the uncertainty of the difference, K is the Kalman gain, and the innovation is measured minus expected. The covariance uses the Joseph form: it’s longer, but it stays symmetric and positive even with rounding errors; the short form (I − K H) Ex can lose symmetry. Testat question: what does icp_gain do, and what value would you choose? Ez is divided by it, so two means trust ICP twice as much. In the simulation with a corridor, a gain of 0.01 ended two metres off, two ended four and a half, and ten almost ten. The gain can’t fix sliding; it only changes how much the filter listens. On the open field, keep it at two or below.
